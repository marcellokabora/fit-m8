Padel has quietly become one of the fastest-growing racket sports around — easier to pick up
than tennis, more social than squash, and built around doubles from day one. But it comes
with one obvious catch: **you need three other people**.

## Why it's exploding

- **Low barrier to entry.** Smaller court, underarm serve, and the walls keep the ball in
  play longer — new players can have a fun rally on their first day.
- **It's inherently social.** Doubles-only means every session is a small group hang, not a
  solo grind.
- **Short, intense sessions.** A match rarely runs much past an hour, which fits into a normal
  week far more easily than a half-day tournament.

![Two pairs playing padel on an indoor court](/blog/why-padel-is-taking-over/footvolley.jpg)

## The real bottleneck: finding a fourth

Booking a court is the easy part. The hard part is lining up three other people who are free
at the same time and roughly your level — a beginner in a match with three experienced
players has a rough time, and so does everyone else on court.

## How to actually solve it

1. **Set your skill level honestly** — padel levels move fast in the first few months, so
   update it as you improve instead of leaving it at "beginner" forever.
2. **Filter by format** — casual weekend padel and competitive league padel attract very
   different players; being clear up front saves a wasted session.
3. **Go nearby first** — the players most likely to become regulars are the ones close enough
   that a Tuesday evening game doesn't feel like a commute.
