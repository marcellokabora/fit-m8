Walk along Barceloneta or Bogatell on a warm evening and you'll hear it before you see it —
the thud of a ball being volleyed back and forth over a net, no hands allowed. Footvolley
(futevôlei) has become one of the city's most visible beach sports, and it's easy to see why.

## What makes footvolley different

- **No hands, ever.** Just feet, chest, head, and thighs — it rewards control and creativity
  over raw power.
- **Small teams, big skill ceiling.** Usually 2v2 on a shortened volleyball-sized court, so
  every touch matters and pickup games stay fast-paced.
- **Beach-native.** Barcelona's long stretch of sand courts means casual nets pop up every
  weekend from spring through autumn, alongside the more organized club courts.

## Where to play in Barcelona

- **Platja de la Barceloneta** — the busiest strip, informal nets fill up fast on weekend
  afternoons.
- **Platja del Bogatell / Nova Icaria** — a bit calmer, popular with regulars who play
  several times a week.
- **Platja de la Mar Bella** — known for a more competitive crowd and pickup runs that go
  late into the evening.

## The catch: you still need a team

Footvolley courts fill up with people who already know each other. Turning up solo — or with
just one friend — usually means waiting on the sand for a spare pair to show, or not playing
at all.

![Two footvolley players in the middle of a rally, mid-air](/blog/footvolley-in-barcelona/action.jpg)

## How to actually get a game going

1. **Be upfront about your level.** Footvolley skews technical fast — knowing a few chest
   traps and volleys versus being a total beginner changes who you should be playing with.
2. **Look for a regular group, not a one-off.** The sport rewards chemistry between partners;
   a squad you play with weekly beats a different pickup team every time.
3. **Go local.** The nearest beach beats the "better" one across town — footvolley is a
   drop-everything-after-work sport, and distance kills that.
